Data Pipeline & Feature Engineering

A rigorous approach to data cleaning, feature extraction, and preprocessing for robust credit risk modeling.

Dataset Overview

We utilize the UCI Credit Card Default Dataset, comprising payment information from 30,000 credit card clients in Taiwan (April-September 2005). Our analysis specifically targets automated decision-making for small loans.

Filtering Criteria

  • Credit Limit: ≤ $200,000 (Small/Consumer Loans)
  • Sample Size: 20,378 customers (67.9% of original)
  • Default Rate: 25.6% (Maintained via stratified sampling)

Data Splits

  • Training: 60% (12,226 samples)
  • Validation: 20% (4,076 samples)
  • Test: 20% (4,076 samples)

Feature Engineering

We engineered 8 temporal and behavioral features to capture credit usage trends and repayment reliability.

Feature Name Description & Business Logic
MAX_DELINQUENCY Worst-case behavior. Maximum payment delay observed across 6 months.
Captures severity of payment issues.
DELINQUENCY_PERSISTENCE Chronic risk. Maximum consecutive months with late payments.
Identifies habituated non-payment behavior.
PAY_SLOPE Payment trend. OLS regression slope of payment amounts over 6 months.
Negative slope may indicate increasing financial stress.
BILL_SLOPE Debt accumulation. OLS regression slope of bill amounts over 6 months.
Positive slope indicates growing debt burden.
UTILIZATION_RATE Credit intensity. Average (Bill Amount / Credit Limit) ratio.
High values indicate reliance on credit limit.
REPAYMENT_RATIO Ability to pay. Total payments / Total bills over 6 months.
Ratio > 1 indicates paying down debt principal.

Preprocessing Pipeline

Glassbox Models (Linear)

Requires strict statistical formatting.

  • One-Hot Encoding: Applied to Sex, Education, Marriage (34 total features).
  • Normalization: Standard scaling applied to continuous features.
  • Class Weighting: 'Balanced' mode to handle 3:1 imbalance.

Blackbox Models (Tree-based)

Optimized for non-linear pattern recognition.

  • Native Categoricals: No one-hot encoding required (31 total features).
  • Raw Data: No normalization needed for decision trees.
  • Loss Function: Cross-Entropy loss for probability calibration.
